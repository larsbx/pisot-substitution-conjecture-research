## Exact proposed claim

For each original substitution `1/021/001` and `1/012/001`, bind a decoded
finite graph to the actual period-two prefix tiles in the unchanged compact
matrix-adic product. Its vertices represent the three zero-offset
distinct-letter roots and their retained inverse ancestry, modulo track
swap. A row slot must retain both parent letters, both literal positions in
the actual second-power words, the resulting integral offset, and whether
normalization swapped the target. Equal endpoint labels do not identify
occurrences.

Prove that complete literal inverse rows, with justified exclusions only,
give a finite cover of each represented geometric overlap. Bind those rows
to the same graph and integer potential accepted by `OverlapContraction`.
The consequence is nullity on that represented rooted domain when its
complete certificate and strict rate gap are actually discharged. This
does not identify the domain with every mathematical `strictPackets`
element, and it supplies no positive strict-root measure.

## Primary sources inspected and hypothesis boundaries

1. **Akiyama and Lee, _Algorithm for determining pure pointedness of
   self-affine tilings_**, [primary preprint, section 4, equations
   (4.1)–(4.2), Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1003.2898), PDF page 12.
   The overlap graph counts all contributing digit pairs, including parallel
   edges. A spectral comparison follows from geometrically faithful
   subdivision. Its pure-point equivalence assumes a self-affine tiling and
   a Meyer return set; the introduction explicitly warns that removing the
   Meyer hypothesis can give nontermination or incorrect output. We reuse
   the complete-multigraph and measure-comparison method, not that
   equivalence or its unproved tiling hypotheses.
2. **Minervino and Thuswaldner, _The geometry of non-unit Pisot
   substitutions_**, [primary preprint, Lemma 3.1 and Theorem
   8.3(i)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.2002), PDF pages 5 and 19. Their
   representation includes non-Archimedean factors; multiplication scales
   its product measure by the reciprocal Perron factor. The tile equation
   is indexed by the prefix automaton, and finite measure comparison uses
   its full occurrence sum. The repository must retain its independently
   constructed `H_M`, actual word, product Haar normalization, and literal
   block equation. No unproved identification with the paper's adelic
   representation, measure-disjointness result, or positive cross-letter
   overlap is transferred.
3. **Thuswaldner, _Unimodular Pisot substitutions and their associated
   tiles_**, [primary publication, Lemma 3.4, Corollary 3.5, Definition
   3.6 and Remark 3.7](https://www.numdam.org/item/JTNB_2006__18_2_487_0.pdf),
   printed pages 507–510. Bounds in the physical and stable coordinates
   supply a finite carrier; monotone closure from specified roots supplies
   its rooted completeness. Canonical ordering requires explicit choices
   about paired-edge orientation. The paper works under unimodularity and
   later uses further tiling hypotheses, unlike the present determinant-two
   examples. Its special identification of certain swapped diagonal edges
   must not replace the repository's positional multiplicities.

The finite weighted-maximum argument, finite lattice bounds, and
graph-directed technique are standard. The intended contribution is the
checked specialization connecting preserved source data and complete
literal addresses to these particular already constructed geometric sets.

## Additional representation obligations

The existing accepted definitions and historical replay leave the following
obligations; a checksum or successful weight inequality alone supplies none
of them.

1. **Source and scale.** Decode the exact substitution and actual
   `sigma^2` words, not just their common incidence matrix. Retain the
   period-two word used to define each tile. Its block-twelve equation
   follows from the existing period argument; one-step invariance is not
   assumed.
2. **Sharp box.** Authenticate every proper-prefix digit of the actual
   `sigma^12` words in the exact named chart and prove coordinate bounds
   `(23/5,14/5)`. Bind the four entry bounds
   `(473,390,147,344)/1000` to that chart's actual `B^12` action. The
   already proved compactness now legitimizes a maximum argument for
   `Q=[-16,16] x [-8,8]`. Alternatively use a complete prefix induction.
   A selected sample, an unbound hull, or the qualitative radius `8D` does
   not prove this sharp box.
3. **Residues and exclusion.** For each actual occurrence pair with prefix
   vectors `p,p'`, use
   `M^2 z_new = z + p' - p`. Prove equivalence of the exact adjugate
   divisibility test with membership in `M^2 Z^3`, using the existing
   matrix-adic range theorem. The other exclusion is strict separation of
   `W z_new` from `Q-Q`. Endpoint contact and uncertain separation remain
   represented. Resource caps are refusal conditions, never emptiness
   proofs.
4. **Rows and orientation.** A normalized slot decodes to a literal inverse
   occurrence with complete parent and local addresses. The ordered lift
   represents both orientations. Require row completeness and absence of
   duplicate address records, not merely that each emitted slot is legal.
   Positional slots with equal targets remain distinct. Establish the
   exact list/count transport before using any matrix transpose.
5. **Ancestry and domain.** Verify all three roots and every decoded row,
   then prove the finite closure includes every nonempty inverse ancestor
   from those roots at every finite depth. An earlier-index discovery
   witness proves reachability of a stored state but does not alone prove
   that all genuine inverse candidates are represented. A graph cap,
   endpoint-only graph, or inverse-closed selected subset is insufficient.
6. **Certificate identity.** Connect the exact decoded ordered rows to the
   checked positive weights and numerator/denominator. The original
   `16,8` graphs have rates `99/20` and `101/20`; the later `24,12`
   eighteen-source graphs have rate `102/20`. Their vertex counts, state
   order, row slots, graph digests and weights remain separate artifacts.
   Prove the relevant strict gap below the same actual `beta^2`.

Once these hypotheses hold on a domain, the literal set cover and accepted
Haar scaling give `beta^2 v <= T v`; accepted positive potentials give
`T w <= lambda w`, with `lambda < beta^2`. The existing
`OverlapContraction.zero_of_subinvariant` or certificate consumer then
proves `v=0`. Its recurrence argument cannot be supplied by the numerical
certificate itself.

## Required negative controls and decision

- Delete a positional edge whose target is repeated: the numerical
  inequality may become easier, but row completeness must fail.
- Reverse the inverse equation, target swap, or physical transpose: reject
  the changed literal address relation even when state counts agree.
- Substitute the `24,12` metadata or use a smaller unsupported box:
  reject source/box/graph identity mismatch, not merely a failed rate.
- Remove an inverse ancestor or stop closure early: reject incomplete
  rows and ancestry while preserving a failed resource receipt.
- Relabel states or reuse another fixture's graph with the same incidence:
  reject the mismatch of actual words, offsets and addresses.
- Retain the inverse-closed but forward-leaking `4 / 2 / 2` calibration.
  Inverse completeness alone does not imply forward conservation.
- Retain nonempty null compact sets and conservative zero-deficit controls:
  no graph equality or conservative component supplies positive strict
  Haar measure.

**Proceed.** Implement the strongest theorem whose hypotheses are fully
spelled out and authenticate as many finite premises as the implementation
can replay. Keep any remaining sharp-box decoder, full graph refinement,
or all-domain coverage as explicit premises. Preserve archived receipts and
failed attempts. C4, BH, A1', G1, Concentration and PSC statuses remain
