### 2.1 What is decided

Theorem P+(2) (rank-two note §7): if `σ ∈ PIP3` has `cr(σ) = 2`, then for
every admissible `n` there are monic `r, q₁ ∈ ℤ[t]` with `r · q₁ | c_n` and
`h_n · r̄ = (t + 1) · q̄₁` in `𝔽₂[t]`. Here `c_n` is the characteristic
polynomial of `(g_Kⁿ)*` on `H¹(ER; ℚ)`, `h_n` the part of `χ_{Mⁿ} mod 2`
prime to `t` (of degree `d₂`), and `n` is admissible if some legal word `ba`
has `g_Rⁿ(b) = b` and `gⁿ(a) = a`. The module `psc.rank_two_parity`
computes these objects as follows.

- *The action on `ER`.* `f = g_K|ER` as a graph map, with the legal words
  and the eventual range reused from `psc.h1_census`: the edge `e_ab` goes
  to `e_{g_R(a) g(b)}`, the vertex `a⁺` to `g_R(a)⁺`, the vertex `b⁻` to
  `g(b)⁻`. `graph_map_on` refuses a disconnected edge set, a map that leaves
  the edge set, a map that is not a permutation of the edges or of the
  vertices, and `n < 1`.
- *Proposition C (proved in the module docstring).* For an
  orientation-preserving automorphism `f` of a finite connected graph and
  `n ≥ 1`,
  `charpoly((fⁿ)* | H¹) = (t − 1) ∏_{edge cycles of fⁿ} (t^L − 1) / ∏_{vertex cycles of fⁿ} (t^L − 1)`.
  Proof: `0 → H⁰ → C⁰ → C¹ → H¹ → 0` is an exact sequence of
  `ℚ[f#]`-modules, characteristic polynomials multiply along it, `H⁰ = ℚ`
  with trivial action because the graph is connected, and on cochains the
  action is a permutation matrix. So the exponent of `Φ_d` in `c_n` is
  `[d = 1] + #{edge cycles of fⁿ with d | L} − #{vertex cycles of fⁿ with d | L}`.
- `c_n` is computed by exact division (a remainder raises) and factored
  twice, from the cycle type and by trial division by every `Φ_d` with
  `φ(d) ≤ deg c_n`; the two must agree and their product must be `c_n`.
- `h_n` comes from `(M mod 2)ⁿ` over `𝔽₂`.
- *The search* runs over every pair of sub-multisets `(a_d)`, `(b_d)` with
  `a_d + b_d ≤ e_d` of `c_n = ∏ Φ_d^{e_d}` and tests
  `h_n · r̄ = (t + 1) · q̄₁`. The docstring argues, by unique factorisation
  in `ℤ[t]` and Gauss's lemma, that these are exactly the monic `r, q₁` the
  theorem allows.
- *Lemma N (proved in the module docstring).* Admissibility of `n`, `c_n`
  and `h_n` depend only on `n mod 42`: the order of `f` on `ER` divides 6
  (checked; the module raises otherwise), and a nonzero eigenvalue of
  `M mod 2` has order dividing 1, 3 or 7. So `n = 1, …, 42` decides "for
  every admissible `n`" exactly, and `n = 6` is always admissible.
- *Import.* Lemma 7(1)–(3) of the (F)-class note. The code checks its finite
  consequences (permutation, connectivity, order dividing 6,
  `deg c_n = dim H¹ − 3`) and refuses on a violation; it does not prove the
  lemma.

### 3.2 The route

### 4.3 The cohomological input at every determinant

**Lemma I (`≈_s` does not depend on the power).** B15 §2 defines

and `Φ^m` with `Φ′`; it and its inverse are uniformly continuous, so they
preserve `∼_s`. `g_max` depends only on the flow. So `≈_s` ("the set of `v`
with `T − v ∼_s T′ − v` is dense, and `g_max(T) = g_max(T′)`", B15 §3) is
the same relation for `Φ`, `Φ^m` and `Φ′`. *Scope (binding correction):*
this holds modulo the proper-version construction, i.e. the isomorphism
conjugating `Φ^m` with `Φ′`, which is by construction from B15 §4; B15
states only "isomorphic ℝ-actions" there, and the rewriting references
behind it (Durand; Barge–Diamond 2001) were not read. With that label,
Lemma I discharges the parenthesis "reconstructed, not checked against the

hold: Pisot family (the dilation is `βⁿ`); border forcing and convex tiles

**Lemma 9″ (Lemma 9′ of the rank-two note in solenoid form; not new: BKS
Corollary 3 and Theorem 5).** For `X = Ω_σ` or `Ω_s`, let `E_X ⊂ ℝ` be the

Thm 5); their hypotheses (a compact minimal tiling space with an ergodic
measure) hold. `E_{Ω_σ} = {u·r : u ∈ Σ*_∞}` by (R2); it has rank 3, since
`Σ*_∞ ⊗ ℚ = ℚ³` and `u ↦ u·r` is injective on `ℚ³` (ranks are dimensions
after `⊗ ℚ`, which is exact). `E_{Ω_s} ⊇ E_{Ω_σ}`, because
`χ_u ∘ h_φ = χ_u ∘ κ⁻¹ ∘ g_s ∘ q` and `χ_u ∘ κ⁻¹ ∘ g_s` is a continuous
eigenfunction on `Ω_s`. `rank [X, S¹] = rank Ȟ¹(X; ℤ)` (BKS §3.2): 3 for

determinant is not.

**Lemma 10 (every determinant; rank-two note §8, not new).** If
`dim H¹ = 3` and `cr ≥ 2`, then `Ω_s` has no homeomorphism `ρ′` of prime

### 4.4 Proof of Theorem Fork

*Step 1 (the quotient pair space).* Put `Z_s := (q × q)(Z)`. It is compact,
flow-invariant and minimal (a continuous equivariant image of a minimal
set). It does not meet the diagonal: if `q(T) = q(T′)` then `T ≈_s T′`, so
`T − v ∼_s T′ − v` for some `v`; by B15 Thm 4(3) there is `k` with
`B₀[Φ^k(T − v)] = B₀[Φ^k(T′ − v)]`, so `Φ^k(T)` and `Φ^k(T′)` share the
tiles containing `β^k v`, against (Z2). By (Z4), (R2) and `g_max = g_s ∘ q`,
`g_s(a) = g_s(b)` for `(a, b) ∈ Z_s`. `π₁(Z_s) = π₂(Z_s) = Ω_s` by
minimality of `Ω_s`.

*Step 2 (`π₁|Z_s` is not injective).* Suppose it were. It is then a
continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space, hence a
homeomorphism, and `ρ_s := π₂ ∘ (π₁|Z_s)⁻¹` is continuous, onto, commutes
with the flow, has no fixed point (Step 1) and satisfies
`g_s ∘ ρ_s = g_s`. The fibres of `g_s` are finite with a uniform bound `K`
(B15 Thm 4(1) for `Φ′_s`, or `g_s⁻¹(z) = q(g_max⁻¹(z))` with BBK Thm 4.2).
`ρ_s` maps each fibre into itself and is onto, so it maps each fibre
bijectively onto itself; so `ρ_s` is a homeomorphism with `ρ_s^{K!} = id`.
Let `N ≥ 2` be its order, `p` a prime dividing `N`, and
`ρ′ := ρ_s^{N/p}`, of order `p`. For `0 < j < p`, `Fix(ρ′^j)` is closed and
flow-invariant, hence empty or `Ω_s`; in the second case `ρ′^j = id`, so
`ρ′ = id` because `gcd(j, p) = 1`, a contradiction. So no nontrivial power
of `ρ′` has a fixed point, and `g_s ∘ ρ′ = g_s`. This contradicts Lemma 10,
whose hypotheses `dim H¹ = 3` and `cr ≥ 2` hold (§4.2).

*Step 3 (lifting).* By Step 2 there are `(a, b)`, `(a, b′) ∈ Z_s` with
`b ≠ b′`. Choose `(T₁, T₁′)`, `(T₂, T₂″) ∈ Z` with `q(T₁) = q(T₂) = a`,
`q(T₁′) = b`, `q(T₂″) = b′`; so `T₁ ≈_s T₂` and `T₁′ ≉_s T₂″`. All four lie
in one fibre of `h_φ`: by (Z4), by "`≈_s` implies equal `g_max`" with (R2),
and by (Z4) again. `U := {v : T₁′ − v ∼_s T₂″ − v}` is open (B15 §3, from
Thm 4(3)) and not dense (else `T₁′ ≈_s T₂″`); choose an open interval `J`
with `J ∩ U = ∅`. `U₁ := {v : T₁ − v ∼_s T₂ − v}` is open and dense; pick
`v₀ ∈ J ∩ U₁` and `k₀` with
`B₀[Φ^{k₀}(T₁ − v₀)] = B₀[Φ^{k₀}(T₂ − v₀)] =: Q`, whose support contains a
neighbourhood `(−ε, ε)` of `0`. Choose `k_i → ∞`, multiples of `P` with
`k_i ≥ k₀`, such that `Φ^{k_i}` of `T₁ − v₀`, `T₂ − v₀`, `T₁′ − v₀`,
`T₂″ − v₀` converge, to `S`, `S₂`, `S′`, `S″`. The patch `Φ^{k_i − k₀}(Q)`
lies in the first two tilings and its support contains
`β^{k_i − k₀}(−ε, ε)`, so `S₂ = S`. `Z` is closed and invariant under the
flow and `Φ^P × Φ^P` (Z1), so `(S, S′)` and `(S, S″)` lie in `Z`.
- *No common tile near `J`, at any level.* For every `m ≥ 0`,
  `Φ^m(T₁′ − v₀)` and `Φ^m(T₂″ − v₀)` share no tile whose interior meets
  `β^m(J − v₀)`: such a tile with interior point `β^m(v − v₀)`, `v ∈ J`,
  would after translation be a common tile of `Φ^m(T₁′ − v)` and
  `Φ^m(T₂″ − v)` with `0` in its interior, `B₀` would agree, and `v ∈ U` by
  Thm 4(3), against `J ∩ U = ∅`.
- *The limits never share a tile.* If `Φⁿ(S′)` and `Φⁿ(S″)` shared a tile
  `t`, then for large `i` the fibre-mates `Φ^{k_i+n}(T₁′ − v₀)` and
  `Φ^{k_i+n}(T₂″ − v₀)` (fibre-mates by (R1)) would contain tiles of the same
  type near `t` with left ends closer than `δ₀`; by (R3) these are one
  shared tile, with an interior point near one of `t`, which lies in
  `β^{k_i+n}(J − v₀)` for large `i` (`J − v₀` is a neighbourhood of `0`).
  This contradicts the previous item.

So `S′` and `S″` never share a tile, and `(S, S′)`, `(S, S″) ∈ Z`.

*Step 4 (spreading).* Let `𝔉` be the set of `(T, T′) ∈ Z` having some `T″`
with `(T, T″) ∈ Z` such that `Φⁿ(T′)` and `Φⁿ(T″)` share no tile for any
`n ≥ 0`. `𝔉` is nonempty (Step 3) and flow-invariant (translate `T″`; use
`Φⁿ(T − t) = Φⁿ(T) − βⁿt`). It is closed: let `(T_j, T_j′) → (T, T′)` with
witnesses `T_j″ → T″` (a subsequence); `(T, T″) ∈ Z` since `Z` is closed;
if `Φⁿ(T′)` and `Φⁿ(T″)` shared a tile, then for large `j` the fibre-mates
`Φⁿ(T_j′)`, `Φⁿ(T_j″)` (both in the fibre of `Φⁿ(T_j)` by (Z4) and (R1))
would have tiles of the same type within `δ₀`, a shared tile by (R3), a
contradiction. By minimality (Z1), `𝔉 = Z`. ∎

### 5.6 A calibration remark (not a statement)

that is not spectral: on two letters `F` has the sign of the orientation of
the pair. No such positivity is known on three letters; it is a candidate
new input, not a result.
