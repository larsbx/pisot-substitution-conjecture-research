## Primary-source check

1. **B.-S. Tam and H. Schneider, On the core of a cone-preserving map,
   Transactions AMS 343(2) (1994), 479--524.**
   [Author PDF](https://people.math.wisc.edu/hans/paper_archive/scanned_papers/hs106.pdf),
   printed p.484, Theorem 2.1 and the preceding definitions. The
   Frobenius--Victory theorem characterizes nonnegative eigenvectors using
   distinguished classes. Its arc convention `i -> j` when `P_ij>0` agrees
   with our row-to-child convention. A distinguished class has Perron root
   strictly larger than every proper ancestor class; its ray is supported
   on vertices that reach it. This is the closest existing theorem.

2. **P. Butkovic, H. Schneider, S. Sergeev and B.-S. Tam, Two cores of a
   nonnegative matrix, Linear Algebra Appl. 439 (2013), 1929--1954.**
   [Author PDF](https://people.math.wisc.edu/hans/paper_archive/other_papers/hs164.pdf),
   section 2.6, equation (6), printed p.1936, and Theorem 6.1, p.1945.
   The ordinary nonnegative-algebra branch confirms the strict ancestor
   comparison and extremal-ray structure. The paper also treats max
   algebra; its weaker comparison must not be imported into the ordinary
   matrix equation used here.

3. **S. Akiyama and J.-Y. Lee, Algorithm for determining pure pointedness
   of self-affine tilings.**
   [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1003.2898), section 4 and
   Theorem 4.1, pp.12--13. Occurrence multiplicities matter in the overlap
   graph. The spectral overlap/PDS criterion assumes an actual self-affine
   tiling and a Meyer set. Those geometric hypotheses are not supplied by
   our finite carrier or by a feasible nonnegative graph eigenvector, so
   no pure-point/tiling conclusion is imported.

4. **M. Minervino and J. M. Thuswaldner, The geometry of non-unit Pisot
   substitutions.**
   [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.2002), Lemma 3.1, p.5;
   Theorem 8.3, p.19; Theorem 9.9, p.24. This supplies the relevant
   full non-unit measure normalization and within-letter occurrence
   disjointness context. A degree-one tiling uses additional finiteness
   and strong-coincidence assumptions. The current proof instead cites
   #497's explicit compact M-adic construction and exact measure equality.

## Transferable conclusion and limits

For an SCC block `A` and its outgoing contribution `b>=0`,
`(theta I-A)v_A=b`. Pairing with a positive left Perron vector yields:

- `rho(A)>theta`: `v_A=0` and all its descendants have zero coordinates.
- `rho(A)=theta`: every strict descendant has zero coordinates; the
  component itself may remain allowed.
- `rho(A)<theta`: its coordinates are determined by outgoing contributions
  through the nonnegative resolvent; a subcritical component need not be zero.

For the whole supplied graph, a critical class is eligible exactly when
all its proper ancestors are strictly subcritical. The nonnegative
`theta`-eigencone is generated by the corresponding upstream rays. This
classifies linear feasibility only. It does not identify the actual
geometric measure vector, which may be zero on an allowed ray.

For integer blocks, invertibility of `g(A)` modulo a prime proves that
`theta` is not an eigenvalue of `A`. It excludes a critical candidate; it
zeros the block only after all outgoing coordinates are already zero.
Failure at a prime is inconclusive. Degree `g` greater than the block size
also excludes that eigenvalue. No floating eigenvalue estimate may decide
acceptance. Modular arithmetic must reuse the vendored prime-field kernel.

## Required controls and bounded experiment

- `theta=2`, `T=[[1,1],[0,2]]`: the first block is noncritical but the
  ray `(a,a)` is allowed. Reject isolated-block zeroing.
- `theta=2`, `T=[[3,1],[0,2]]`: a supercritical ancestor kills the
  downstream critical component and the whole nonnegative eigencone.
- `theta=2`, `T=[[2,1],[0,2]]`: the upstream critical block kills the
  downstream critical block; only `(a,0)` remains.
- `theta=2`, `T=[[3,1],[1,3]]`: an algebraic theta eigenvalue has only
  signed eigenvectors; polynomial singularity does not establish Perron
  criticality.
- For the actual cubic `g`, `A=[1]` has `g(A)=[-5]`: modulo 5 is a bad
  prime, not a criticality certificate.
- Removing external incoming equations can enlarge the feasible cone.
  Authenticate the complete chosen graph and its SCC decomposition;
  never claim geometric positivity from a root-reachable restriction.
- New supercritical/critical rules require the exact equality. An older
  one-sided upper-bound inequality is insufficient.
- Preserve all occurrence multiplicities; corrupted rows, partition data,
  modular pivots/witnesses and resource exhaustion must refuse acceptance.

The initial finite experiment uses the existing complete twelve-source
class, deterministic SCCs, exact finite-field polynomial certificates and
explicit matrix-size/prime budgets. Report every unresolved block rather
than silently falling back to the previous weight result. Synthetic
controls establish increased linear-system scope; they are not Pisot
substitution counterexamples. A missing successful certificate remains
inconclusive, and no universal critical-component exclusion is proposed.
