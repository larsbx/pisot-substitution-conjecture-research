## 1. Full-rank phases and integral image lattices

Let sigma have primitive *irreducible cubic Pisot* incidence M in Z^(3x3), beta>1, and nonzero determinant m=det M. Let C be a **supplied finite, strongly connected, fully strict child-closed component** of *actual* irreducible balanced pairs, with no diagonal or omitted occurrence. Write P for its integral Parikh mass matrix and N for the complete occurrence-indexed child incidence (row child, column parent). Thus PN=MP. Let N have graph period h, and let C_k be its cyclic classes, with all children of C_k in C_(k+1). Set P_k to the columns of P in C_k.

As in PR #484, applying the identity for h generations gives
\`P_k (N^h)|_(C_k) = M^h P_k\`.
The Perron eigenvalue beta^h has degree three: its two other conjugates have moduli <1 and cannot equal beta^h, so beta^h is not rational; a cubic number field has no proper nontrivial subfields. Hence chi_(M^h) is irreducible cubic and \`rank_Q(P_k)=3\`.

**Definition (integral phase lattice).** Let
\`L_k = P_k Z^(|C_k|) subset Z^3\`. Since P_k has rank three, L_k is a full-rank sublattice. Its index
\`g_k=[Z^3:L_k]\` is the **positive gcd of every 3x3 maximal minor of P_k** (integer Smith normal form). The index is independent of column order or repeated state masses. Do not replace it by a single chosen triple unless each phase has exactly three states.

## 2. Exact lattice-flow theorem

**Theorem 1 (phase index conservation).** Under §1, for each k modulo h,

~~~text
M L_k subseteq L_(k+1),
t_k := [L_(k+1) : M L_k]
     = |m| g_k / g_(k+1)  is a positive INTEGER,
product_(k=0)^(h-1) t_k = |m|^h.
~~~

For every rational prime p not dividing m, the p-primary Smith invariant factors of \`Z^3/L_k\` are independent of k. Equivalently, the localized lattices satisfy
\`L_(k+1) tensor Z_p = M (L_k tensor Z_p)\`
for all p not dividing m. Every transition index t_k has prime factors **only** among the prime divisors of m.

In the **unimodular case** \`|m|=1\`, necessarily
\`t_k=1\`, \`g_0=...=g_(h-1)\`, and

~~~text
L_(k+1) = M L_k  as ACTUAL integral lattices.
~~~

These identities are stronger than equality of Perron-weighted real masses across phases: they record the exact integral subgroup generated by *all* state Parikh vectors.

**Proof.** Every complete child occurrence from a state in C_k lies in C_(k+1). Restrict PN=MP to the corresponding parent columns: each vector M p_j is an integer linear combination of columns P_(k+1). Hence M L_k subseteq L_(k+1), with both sides full rank. Integer M has det m != 0 and the lattice M L_k has ambient index |m|g_k. For full-rank lattice inclusions A subseteq B subseteq Z^3, [B:A]=[Z^3:A]/[Z^3:B]. Thus t_k is the displayed positive integer. Multiply the h identities and cancel the g_k cyclically to obtain \`product t_k=|m|^h\`. Therefore no t_k can have a prime factor outside m. Localizing the finite-index inclusion at p not dividing m kills its finite quotient, proving equality of the p-local lattices. Since M is invertible over Z_p, it carries the p-primary quotient of L_k isomorphically to that of L_(k+1), so their p-Smith invariants agree. If |m|=1, the product of positive integers t_k equals one; hence each t_k=1, giving actual lattice equality and constant g_k. QED.

**Corollary 2 (prime-adic phase budget).** For any p dividing |m|, let a=v_p(|m|). Then
\`e_k=v_p(t_k)=a+v_p(g_k)-v_p(g_(k+1))>=0\` and
\`sum_k e_k=h a\`. The primes p not dividing m have constant v_p(g_k) on all phases. For determinant 2 and period two, the ONLY possible transition-index allocations are \`(t_0,t_1)=(1,4),(2,2),(4,1)\`. This is an exact arithmetic restriction, not a claim that all three are word-realizable.

## 3. Equality-size determinant obstruction

**Proposition 3 (exactly 3h states).** If a supplied strict component has \`|C|=3h\`, each cyclic phase has exactly three states and its P_k is a 3x3 rationally invertible *integer* matrix. Let
\`A_k=N_(C_(k+1),C_k)\` be the complete **literal** 3x3 one-step transition block. Then

~~~text
P_(k+1) A_k = M P_k,
det(A_k) = m det(P_k) / det(P_(k+1)) != 0,
|det A_k| = t_k,
product_k det A_k = m^h.
~~~

Thus **every transition block is nonsingular**, not just the h-step return block; in the unimodular case each A_k is a nonnegative integral **unimodular** matrix with det ±1. Any proposed r=3h SCC with a singular phase-to-phase 3x3 child incidence is excluded immediately.

**Proof.** The restricted intertwining is the displayed matrix equality. Both P_k are invertible and m != 0, so A_k is invertible. Taking determinants gives its formula, which agrees with Theorem 1 because g_k=|det P_k| for a square phase. Multiply around the cycle to obtain det M^h. QED.

**Why this does not finish C4:** These are necessary *integer* tests. A nonnegative P_k can consist of basis columns that cannot be masses of noncoincident irreducible pairs; even requiring each column to contain all three letters does not imply the child rows arise from a genuine substitution. Conversely, positive-dimensional Rauzy subtile overlaps from #480–#485 do not imply the literal synchronized newborn in Issue #461. No geometric-disjointness or one-pair primed-to-unprimed argument is imported.

## 4. Exact saturations and negative controls (non-word systems!)

Let
\`M_2=[[1,0,1],[1,2,1],[0,1,1]]\` be the PIP nonunit incidence of the forward run family (det=2, M_2^2>0).

For period h=2, both following **synthetic** block packets satisfy PN=MP and have r=6 and full rank in both phases:

* \`P=[I,M_2]\`, \`N=[[0,M_2^2],[I,0]]\`: phase indices (g_0,g_1)=(1,2), and transitions (t_0,t_1)=(1,4).
* \`P=[M_2^2,M_2^2]\`, \`N=[[0,M_2],[M_2,0]]\`: phase indices (4,4), and transitions (2,2). Every P-column has **positive** 0-,1-,2-coordinates, so a mere Parikh-support test cannot reject this synthetic packet.

The (4,1) allocation results by interchanging the two phase roles of the first packet. These controls prove the prime-adic allocation constraint is **algebraically sharp**, not that any packet is a literal balanced-pair SCC.

For a **unit** control, take the Tribonacci incidence
\`M_1=[[1,1,1],[1,0,0],[0,1,0]]\`, det=1, irreducible Pisot. With \`P=[M_1^3,M_1^3]\` and \`N=[[0,M_1],[M_1,0]]\`, all six synthetic P columns have support on all three letters, both lattice indices are one, and both transition blocks are unimodular. This demonstrates that positive mass support and unimodular phase transport alone do **not** exclude a synthetic strict template.

Negative controls: a broken PN=MP equation, incorrect phase edges, singular period phase P, or an incorrect t_k allocation must be refused. The real four-state strict **reducible-characteristic** control (10/102/211) has global rank two and is not an admissible PIP phase packet.
