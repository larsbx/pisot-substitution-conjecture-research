## B. Mathematics re-derived here

### B.1 Proposition 1, overlap depth gives bounded gaps (Prop 5.46)

*If every vertex of the seed-patch overlap graph is productive and `D` is the
largest first-coincidence depth, every state reached at level `n >= D` has
geometric length at most `2 β^D ℓ_max`; hence `B_σ` is finite.*

**Correct.** The argument: overlaps of the level-`(n−D)` tilings scaled by
`β^D` cover the interval; the one containing `t` has `|R| <= β^D ℓ_max`;
productivity gives a coincidence descendant at depth exactly `D` (descendants
of a coincidence are coincidences), which is a common tile inside `R`; its
endpoints are common vertices, hence balanced cuts because `Q`-independence of
`ℓ` makes equal geometric length equal Parikh. So every point is within
`β^D ℓ_max` of a cut, consecutive cuts are at most twice that apart, and each
level-`n` piece is a state. Finiteness then follows from bounded symbolic
length plus finitely many sub-`D` levels.

Two things make this sound that are easy to get wrong, and the note gets both
right. The hypothesis is productivity of **every** swap seed, not the one-seed
gate of Theorem 5.38, and §2 says so. And §1 justifies treating finite-patch
overlaps as vertices of the periodic-patch graph by observing that both
periodic patches have a boundary at the right end of the first period, so no
overlap straddles it.

This does not contradict the standing remark that Theorem 4.22 alone does not
give G1 (`manuscripts/…tex`, after Theorem 4.22). The new input is
*productivity* with its finite depth, not finiteness of the graph.

The mechanism is Sirvent–Solomyak (2002) Theorem 5.6, and §0 credits it
explicitly, claiming only the transfer to the repository's possibly illegal
swap seeds with an explicit bound. That is the right novelty boundary.

### B.2 Proposition 4 and Corollary 5, the half-coincidence strengthening (Prop 5.47)

**Correct, and the sharper of the two.** Proposition 4 replaces the
coincidence by an offset-zero descendant: the two tiles then share a left
endpoint `y`, inflation preserves tile endpoints, so `y` survives as a common
vertex at level `n` and Lemma 5.30 makes it a balanced cut. Since a
coincidence has offset zero, `K' <= D`.

Corollary 5 is the structurally interesting part. With `Z` the reachable
vertices having no offset-zero descendant: `Z` is child-closed (a child with
such a descendant would give the parent one); every vertex of `Z` is
nonproductive (productive gives a coincidence, which is offset zero); and
conversely any closed case-(b) set lies in `Z`. So Proposition 4's hypothesis
holds exactly when no reachable closed nonproductive set is a strict zipper.

**Consequence, which I confirm:** G1 needs only the strict-zipper branch
(#139). The aligned branch (#138, strong coincidence) is needed for
productivity and PDS but **not** for finiteness. That is a real decoupling of
two problems the program had treated as one gate.

### B.4 Theorem B, and why it does not breach the realization firewall

This was my main concern going in. The `PDS ⇒ G1` chain needs to turn a
*formal* strict zipper into *actual* tilings, and the hypothesis firewall
forbids exactly that shortcut ("formal recurrence does not imply global
realization").

**The firewall holds.** Theorem B does not realize the illegal swap seed. It
takes interior occurrences `σ^r(i) = P i U`, `σ^r(j) = Q j V` and builds the
`Φ^r`-fixed tilings `T(i,P)`, `T(j,Q)`; every patch of those is a subpatch of
some `σ^{rn}(i)`, hence allowed for `σ`, so they lie in `Ω_σ`. The offset
`w_0 = (M^r − I)^{-1}(π(P) − π(Q))` is integral not by assumption but because
it *is* the offset of an actual graph vertex (manuscript Lemma 5.45), and
`M^r − I` is invertible since no conjugate of `β` is a root of unity. The
converse direction gets its interior occurrences from Lemma C, which is where
the real work sits. The common-centre and `r`-cycle steps are straightforward;
the equivalence (a)⟺(b)⟺(c) is the boundary-hitting criterion (H) plus the
observation that every common vertex is seen at some depth `rn`.

I did not re-derive Lemma C, Proposition F, Theorem R, Proposition V or
Proposition FP. They are the remaining load-bearing links (§C).

## C. The `PDS ⇒ G1` chain, as recorded

Manuscript `prop:PDS-implies-G1` (`\Status{Theorem}`) and `thm:seedwise`
(answering former Open Problem 4.24) assemble:

```text
reachable strict zipper
  -> Φ^r-fixed pair, common interior centre, integral offset, no common vertex   [Thm B, Cor B′]
  -> the two tilings lie in one MEF fibre                                        [Prop F, Thm R]
  -> no common tile => coincidence rank >= 2 => not PDS                          [IMPORTED: Barge]
  => PDS excludes strict zippers from every seed
  -> G1                                                                          [Prop 5.47 = Cor 5]
```

The import is audited in `docs/coincidence-rank-imports-literature-gate-2026-10-04.md`,
and that gate is good work: it checks Barge (arXiv:1301.7094 Thm 4;
arXiv:1505.04408 §1 items (1)–(3)) and finds **no unimodularity and no
irreducibility** required, so the non-unit PIP regime is covered, and it
explicitly refuses the unimodular-only Barge–Gambaudo realization. It also
states its own limit in §4: item (3) is taken from a published
*introduction's summary*, whose proofs the gate did not read. That is a
thinner basis than the Barge–Štimac–Williams import, which I verified against
its actual Section 3 on 2026-09-21, and it should be recorded as such rather
than left inside one gate note.
