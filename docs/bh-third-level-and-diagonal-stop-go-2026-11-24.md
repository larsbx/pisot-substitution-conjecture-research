# Source review for the third-level entry and diagonal band

The previous two-band proof reduces entry to two actual third-level marks
of type 1 whose interiors overlap by at least a quarter of their tile
length. Proceed with a finite three-window selection in the shared
inflated interval. Each track has at most one third-level image of a
type 2 parent in the entire window union, and one such image is too
short to meet two separated windows. This replaces the open-component
length argument in the written proof with an elementary finite choice.
Bind both selected marks by original image ancestry.

[Sirvent and Solomyak, Sections 4–5](https://doi.org/10.4153/CMB-2002-062-3)
give the one-dimensional overlap framework. [Barge and Diamond,
Section 2](https://www.numdam.org/item/?id=BSMF_2002__130_4_619_0)
illustrate why coincidence certificates use actual letters and abelianized
prefixes. The hypotheses of the spectral criterion in the earlier scope
are unchanged. These papers leave the explicit third-level ordinal
selection to derive.

For the separate band k=r, proceed with a uniform sufficient region in
the two last integral coordinates. The permitted update can either
rotate a pair by a quarter turn or decrease one absolute coordinate.
On a coordinate axis first use a child with the other rounding. The
unit-axis case may use the literal last 2 and first run 1, then the
two original first letters. The two-band quadratic threshold alone
does not supply this exit. Relate scalar admissibility to the
length of the actual image run at each step.

[Akiyama et al. on shift radix systems](https://www.math.tsukuba.ac.jp/~akiyama/papers/cubic.pdf)
and [Akiyama and Pethő on discretized rotation](https://doi.org/10.1088/0951-7715/26/3/871)
study prescribed rounding maps and periodic orbits. The present control
selects from several literal digits and can leave the type 1/1 region.
A periodic orbit of one choice is only a refusal control for that choice.
In particular check an exact four-step nearest-rounding orbit and a
literal exit from its small coordinate-axis region. A complete
classification outside this region remains a separate goal.
