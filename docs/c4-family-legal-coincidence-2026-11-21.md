# Dense eventual coincidence on legal tiling patches

## Source decision

Proceed with the one-dimensional patch-transfer step of
[Barge–Štimac–Williams, Theorem 3.1](https://doi.org/10.3934/dcds.2013.33.579)
on the literal `LengthSubst` hull. The target is density on the open
overlap of two legal patches sharing a tile. The two periodic swap tracks,
their dense eventual coincidences, and their irrational translation ratio
are available from [the family geometry](c4-family-tiling-2026-11-21.md).

[Barge](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.24033/bsmf.2691.pdf)
relates regional proximality and coincidence rank to the maximal
equicontinuous factor. The present geometric step can be checked before using
those structural and measure-theoretic results. The overlap graph theorem of
[Akiyama–Lee](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2010.07.019) gives another route
through overlap coincidence for self-affine tilings. The periodic swap proof
uses the single supplied pair and applies to these determinant-two systems.

At each level take the interior of the region covered by common tiles.
In particular, a point can be inside a common tile and land on a descendant
seam after inflation. For a unit-length Thue–Morse tile at zero, the midpoint
lands at the boundary between the two children. If both tracks contain the
parent, the children on both sides of that boundary remain common. This is
why the proof uses the interior of the entire common region at a level.

## Work shown

### Assumptions and hypotheses

For a length substitution on a finite nonempty alphabet, a legal patch
embeds by translation in a supertile. The transfer uses a positive power
in which every letter occurs in every image and a periodic tiling with a
dense eventual-coincidence set for one irrational shift relative to its
period. The literal family supplies these inputs for every `k ≥ 2`.

### Derivation / reduction

1. At a common inflation depth the middle tiling has a unique tile at each
   point. This gives transitivity on neighborhoods. Inflation preserves
   coverage by common tiles, and the existing window-agreement law gives
   locality of eventual coincidence.
2. For a displacement `w`, let `U` be the open eventual-coincidence set
   between a periodic tiling and its translate by `w`. Intersect `U`
   with the open dense sets for the generator shift at `x` and `x+w`.
   Transitivity sends points in that intersection to `U` after adding the
   generator. The reverse generator has the same property by symmetry
   and translation. The closure of `U` is therefore invariant under the
   dense subgroup generated by the period and generator, so it is all
   of the line whenever `U` is inhabited.
3. Embed two legal patches as subpatches of two translates of the same
   inflated periodic tiling. A shared tile provides an interior point
   in `U`. On the open overlap the full tilings agree with the embedded
   patches, so the dense coincidence set restricts to that overlap.
   Windows in legal hull tilings extend the result to the whole line
   for any two legal tilings with a common tile.
4. Equality of closed origin patches `B₀` implies exact agreement on
   a small open window, including at a seam. The two witnesses in the
   published proof share a tile at their common translated position.
   Dense eventual coincidence of the witnesses gives a coincidence
   of the periodic tilings on their origin window. Advance to a
   common multiple of their inflation periods to find a shared tile
   in the original periodic tilings.
5. The previously constructed `1.1` tiling is fixed by squared inflation.
   For an injective inflation semiconjugate to a factor endomorphism
   with finite fibres, every tiling in its fibre has an inflation
   period. If equality in the fibre supplies the two-point witnesses,
   every pair of tilings in that fibre shares a tile.

### Negative controls

The Lean audit checks that the midpoint of the common unit Thue–Morse tile
lands at a child seam. After inflation the point remains interior to the
region covered by common tiles, while no individual child contains it in
its interior. The earlier finite-factor controls in the family gate also run.

### Non-claims / remaining gaps

For a Lean spectral conclusion the concrete hull still needs the
recognizability and finite-to-one maximal equicontinuous factor results
used in Theorem 3.1, including the implication from same-fibre membership
to the closed-origin witnesses. The conditional fibre theorem exposes
those inputs. The remaining spectral step uses the disjoint-pair criterion
for absence of pure discrete spectrum, or the corresponding characterization
of coincidence rank one and its invariant-measure `L²` conclusion. The
written family spectrum proof continues to use the published Corollary 2.
