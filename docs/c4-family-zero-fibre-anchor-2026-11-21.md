## Derivation

Write `T ≈ U` when the eventual-coincidence set `E(T,U)` is dense. `E` is
open, and `E(P,Q) ∩ E(Q,R) ⊆ E(P,R)` for a tiling `Q`.

1. **Transitivity** (`dense_eventual_trans`). Two dense open sets meet in
   a dense set, so `P ≈ Q ≈ R ⟹ P ≈ R`.
2. **The coincidence shifts form a group** (`coincidenceShifts`). Let
   `H(T) = {w : T ≈ T − w}`. Translation equivariance of `E` gives
   `T − a ≈ T − a − b` from `b ∈ H(T)`; with step 1, `a + b ∈ H(T)`.
   Negation is translation by `−a` and symmetry. Zero uses the hypothesis
   on `T, T`.
3. **Return vectors** (`return_mem`). If `(a,x), (a,y) ∈ T`, then `T` and
   `T − (y−x)` share the tile `(a,x)`, so `y − x ∈ H(T)` by the legal-patch
   theorem.
4. **Inflation** (`inflate_mem`). `w ∈ H(T) ⟹ βw ∈ H(ΦT)`, because
   `Φ(T − w) = ΦT − βw` and inflation preserves density of `E`.
5. **Tile lengths** (`length_mem_coincidenceShifts`). Every hull tiling
   contains a translate of each level-one supertile
   (`contains_image_patch`: a level-three supertile sits in `T`, and each
   letter occurs in each level-two supertile). The image `001` has two
   tiles of type `0` at distance `ℓ₀`; for `k ≥ 2` the image `0 1ᵏ 2` has
   two tiles of type `1` at distance `ℓ₁`. So `ℓ₀, ℓ₁ ∈ H(T)` for every
   `T`. Write `T = ΦT'`. Then `βℓ₁ ∈ H(T)` by step 4, and
   `ℓ₂ = βℓ₁ − ℓ₀ − kℓ₁` (`length_two_eq`, the cubic for `β`).
6. **Starts in one tiling** (`start_sub_mem_lengthGroup`). Two tile starts
   of one legal tiling lie in one window that is a translate of a supertile
   patch, and placed tiles start at sums of tile lengths. Their difference
   is in the group `L` generated by the tile lengths.
7. **Lattice cosets** (`dense_eventual_of_lattice_offset`). Take tiles
   `(1,x) ∈ T`, `(1,y) ∈ U`. If `x(T) − x(U) ∈ ℓ·ℤ³ ⊆ L`, step 6 gives
   `x − y ∈ L ⊆ H(T)`. Then `T ≈ T − (x−y)`, and `T − (x−y)` shares
   `(1,y)` with `U`; so `T ≈ U`.
8. **Same image** (`lattice_offset_of_realize_eq`). Equal images have
   equal zeroth torus coordinate, so the vector lifts differ by an integer
   vector `v`, and `ℓ·G(T) = −x(T)` gives `x(T) − x(U) = ℓ·v`.
9. **Periodicity** (`periodic_common_tile_of_lattice_offset`). A point of
   `E(T,U)` gives a common tile of `Φⁿ T, Φⁿ U` at some depth, hence at a
   common multiple of the two periods, which returns `T` and `U`.
10. **Zero fibre.** Over zero both tilings are periodic with origin starts
    in `−ℓ·ℤ³` by the existing characterization; apply 7 and 9.

Steps 1–4 and 6 are generic (`PeriodicPartnerReturnModule.lean`), with the
legal-patch theorem as an explicit hypothesis. Steps 5 and 7–10 are the
family module.

## Negative controls

- `one_pair_absent_at_one`: at `k = 1` the second type-`1` tile of the
  image of `1` does not exist. The guard `k ≥ 2` in step 5 is used, in
  addition to its use in `hull_dense_eventual`.
- `same_fibre_distinct_control`: for every `k ≥ 2` there are distinct
  tilings with equal image, different closed origin patches and no direct
  radius-one agreement, which are nevertheless densely eventually
  coincident and share a tile. The theorems give neither injectivity nor
  equality.
- The generic module keeps "sharing a tile implies dense eventual
  coincidence" as a hypothesis. The table substitution in
  Barge–Štimac–Williams Example 4.5 shows that it can fail.
- The 48 audit refusals.

## Non-claims and remaining gaps

- `H(T)` is not identified. Only `ℓ·ℤ³ ⊆ H(T)` is proved; for a nonunit
  substitution the full set can be larger.
- `fibre_dense_eventual` is not the published coincidence-rank-one
  statement, not proximality of an arbitrary same-fibre pair, and not a
  common tile without periodicity.
- The map is not identified in Lean with the maximal equicontinuous
  factor.
- Almost-everywhere singleton fibres and the invariant-measure `L²`
  conclusion remain unformalized. They are the next obligations.
- Nothing is asserted for `k = 1`, for other substitutions, or for the
  universal C4 program.
- Independent human review is pending.
