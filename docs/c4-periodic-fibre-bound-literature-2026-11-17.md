# Literature check for counting larger periodic fibres

The proposed estimate counts points fixed by the toral action whose full
legal fibre has three or more tilings. The count should grow strictly
more slowly than the inflation rate. It must include fibres on which
inflation permutes the tilings. The experiment uses ordered triples of
actual overlapping tiles and keeps every occurrence in the graph.

| Source | Scope used here |
| --- | --- |
| [Baker–Barge–Kwapisz, Proposition 2.3, Definition 3.1, Definition 4.1, Theorem 4.2 and Remark 4.3](https://www.numdam.org/article/AIF_2006__56_7_2213_0.pdf) | Bounded stable coordinates, full lattice in the irreducible case, periodic fibre actions, uniform fibre size and the obstruction to three pairwise noncoincident strands at rank two. The Haar-generic cardinality supplies no periodic counting estimate by itself. |
| [Barge, Theorem 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1301.7094) | Every legal fibre has a disjoint family of size equal to coincidence rank. A fibre of exactly that size is pairwise noncoincident. |
| [Akiyama–Lee, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1003.2898) | The overlap-width and spectral-gap mechanism is standard. Here it is applied to three literal tracks, discarding a child as soon as any two central tiles agree. Their overlap-coincidence theorem is not imported as a rank-two exclusion. |
| [Barge–Diamond–Swanson, introduction and §1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0804.0930) | The branch locus is defined from periodic asymptotic tilings. Asymptoticity is not imposed on the fibres counted here. |

Proceed with the triple-overlap estimate and the permutation-aware count.
Stop on a leading-order shortage if both the fixed-pair supply and the
strict return demand have the same leading coefficient. Use the explicit
two-sheet toral cover as a control for that inference, and retain the
zero base point separately because its periodic lifts can have a vertex
at the origin.
